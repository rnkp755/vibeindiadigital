## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are musicians, singers, lyricists, and independent release teams who want to distribute their message through streaming platforms. They arrive with a concrete workflow in mind: sign in, create a release order, upload assets, complete payment, and track progress without confusion.

### Brand Personality
The interface should feel modern, music-led, and Gen Z without becoming gimmicky. It should project momentum, confidence, and creative energy while still feeling trustworthy enough for payments and release management.

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark mode only. The product should feel like a polished backstage console for music releases: atmospheric, rhythmic, and current. Color and microinteractions are part of the "aha" moment, but they must support clarity rather than overpower it. Explicit anti-reference: do not drift into a light-theme SaaS aesthetic.

### Design Principles
1. Make the release flow obvious: every major screen should reinforce auth, upload, payment, and status tracking.
2. Use color as a controlled signal: high-contrast accents for action, status, and energy, not generic gradients everywhere.
3. Favor editorial rhythm over repeated card grids: clear hierarchy, asymmetric composition, and breathing room.
4. Motion should feel like feedback from a music tool: smooth, quick, and performance-safe, with reduced-motion fallbacks.
5. Copy should sound direct and creator-focused: practical language, low jargon, and visible next steps.
